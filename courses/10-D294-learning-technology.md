**Time:** 15 calendar days · ~20 hours on WGU work · a few hours of outside courses *(from my study journal; see [Time per course](../README.md#time-per-course))*

**How it went:** Four short papers, about 5 hours each. By this point I was splitting each night between WGU, pwn.college, and Python. The last paper was approved on August 5, and that was the degree.

This course very much felt like a lower level course that covered a required section on education. It reminded me of undergrad courses I took for other disciplines. I love education so it's fine.

## In one line

Four research papers on how technology changes learning: an AI-flashcard trend analysis, a comparison of three language-learning tools, a risk analysis of generative AI in classrooms, and an LMS analytics comparison.

## What the course covers

Researching trends in emerging learning technology, judging whether tools support deep learning and different learners, and analyzing the safety, legal, and ethical issues of putting them in classrooms and training programs.

## What I did

1. **Emerging trend: AI-generated adaptive retrieval practice.** Traced flashcards from the Leitner box system (1970s) to LLM-generated, auto-scheduled practice items. Looked at what practitioners on a spaced-repetition forum are saying, the research on retention, and the equity risks: paywalled premium tiers and training-data bias.
2. **Tool evaluation for one learning goal** (workplace conversational fluency in a second language). Compared **Anki** (SM-2 vs. FSRS scheduling, AI add-ons), **Duolingo Max** (AI role-play, "Explain My Answer"), and **Rosetta Stone** (speech-recognition feedback). Covered deep learning, adaptivity, accessibility, and LMS integration.
3. **Issues analysis: generative AI in the classroom.** *Safety:* weaker competence in high-stakes fields. *Legal:* copyright liability, and the fact that AI-only work can't be copyrighted under current U.S. Copyright Office guidance. *Ethical:* academic integrity and "cognitive surrender."
4. **LMS learning analytics: Canvas vs. Google Classroom.** What data each system collects, its analytics features, and how to use them responsibly.

## Skills used

## What I'd do differently

- **Write to the length asked.** My papers ran 4–7 pages when 1–2 would pass. One task could have been a single flowchart. Know when you're done.
- **Lead with the recommendation.** Each paper should open with one sentence: "Use X for Y because Z."

## Key ideas worth knowing

- Spaced repetition; the Leitner system; SM-2 vs. FSRS; retrieval practice
- Personal Learning Networks (PLNs); Open Educational Resources (OER)
- Adaptive learning; extended reality (XR) in training
- LMS features; LTI (Learning Tools Interoperability); SCORM / xAPI
- Learning analytics: descriptive → predictive → prescriptive
- Universal Design for Learning (UDL); accessibility in learning platforms

## Books and reading

### *How People Learn: Brain, Mind, Experience, and School* (expanded ed.)
**National Research Council** (J. D. Bransford, A. L. Brown, R. R. Cocking, eds.) · 2000 · National Academies Press · [free to read](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/)

**What's in it:** what learning research says about experts vs. novices, transfer, the learning environment, and technology.

**Thoughts:** I have read a few variations of this book's contents. I skimmed it. You can skip this unless relevant to your chosen paper topics.

### *Learning Management Systems*
**EdTech Books** (open textbook) · [free](https://edtechbooks.org/learning_management_systems)

**What's in it:** LMS definitions, features, and types; stakeholders and usability; selecting, implementing, and evaluating an LMS.

**Thoughts:** LMS are a critical component of modern formal education and a topic for one of the papers. Useful to skim or understand as a topic. 

### *Teaching in a Digital Age* (2nd ed.)
**A. W. (Tony) Bates** · 2019 · BCcampus · [free](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/teachinginadigitalagev2/)

**What's in it:** how teaching changes with technology: online and blended learning, MOOCs, choosing media, and designing quality digital courses.

**Thoughts:** I enjoyed this book as it was a bit more topical. This is 2019 version but there is a 2022 version. I enjoyed the topics so I read but more for my own pleasure. It is interesting as a topic, but this book's value to you is very dependent on your topic choices and paper selection.
